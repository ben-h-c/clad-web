He describes it as creating nationwide standards for NIL compensation, revenue sharing, athlete transfers, recruitment, and agent rules to replace the current mix of state laws, court rulings, and conference policies. Crapo argues this is needed to prevent only the wealthiest programs from surviving and to protect smaller programs, Olympic sports, and women's sports from being crushed by escalating costs.\n\nThe segment also addresses the bill's path forward: the House is not in session until after the November midterms (returning Nov. 9), leading to uncertainty on timing. Crapo expresses optimism that the House will pass it or accept it with minor changes, citing strong public interest in college sports. No external guests; sourcing is the senator's own perspective referencing the legislation and general predictions about its impacts."
