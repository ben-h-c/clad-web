summary: "This hearing segment features Rep. Emily Randall (D-WA) testifying before the House Natural Resources Subcommittee on Indian and Insular Affairs in support of her bill H.R. 7269, the Helping Our People Act of 2026 (Lushootseed title: kʷaxʷad tiiɫ ʔiišədčəɫ). She recounts the Puyallup Tribe's history as a signatory to the Treaty of Medicine Creek, its successful litigation over stolen lands and riverbed title, and the resulting 1989 Settlement Act that restored land, created a $22 million permanent trust fund for social services, and established a trust board. Randall argues the board now imposes unnecessary administrative costs that consume most investment income, and that post-1994 law enables tribal self-management. The bill would amend the 1989 Act to allow the tribe to withdraw the fund under a Secretary-approved plan per the American Indian Trust Fund Management Reform Act, converting it to better support education, social services, and priorities chosen by the tribe. She introduces tribal Councilwoman Annette Bryan for further testimony.\n\nSourcing is primarily the bill text, the 1989 statute, the 1994 Reform Act, and the tribe's own account of burdens; no external experts or opposing views appear in the clip. assessment: "The testimony is factually accurate on the history and legal framework. The 1989 Act did establish an inviolate $22 million principal (only income spendable on listed social services) administered by a board separate from tribal government. Tribal witnesses have documented that low returns and board overhead now leave little for direct services, a problem the 1994 Act was designed to solve for other tribes by allowing withdrawal under approved investment plans. Viewers might miss that the principal remains protected and that the bill requires Secretarial approval of a management plan, so it is not unrestricted spending. The sovereignty rhetoric is standard in Indian Affairs but frames the issue as inherent rights versus federal bureaucracy without noting the original settlement was a negotiated compromise involving multiple parties to clear title for Tacoma development. Overall high-quality, narrowly focused legislative advocacy with strong primary sourcing."
